# CHAPTER 3
## RESEARCH DESIGN AND METHODOLOGY

This chapter describes how QuakeStudio was built and how it is verified and tested. The study uses an experimental and developmental research design: a simplified structural model is formulated under adviser-reviewed assumptions, implemented as a browser application, cross-checked against an independent reference implementation of the same model, and then subjected to parameter testing, software testing, and a deferred usability protocol. All procedures are reported within the stated simplifications; no procedure assesses the safety of a real building.

### Research Design

The design has two linked components. The developmental component produces the interactive 3D building model and integrates the selected structural and seismic factors into the platform. The experimental component conducts controlled scenario-based simulations in which one factor is varied at a time under the same ground-motion input and the resulting ratings-independent outputs — peak displacement, displacement over time, maximum drift, and natural periods — are compared.

Earthquake conditions are represented through synthetic earthquake data (preset, peak ground acceleration, frequency where applicable, duration, and seed), and the building through material, frame type, floor count, floor height, and plan dimensions. Soil type, foundation type, and fault distance are not experimental factors in the prototype; they are reserved as future scope pending engineering approval and appear in the interface only as disabled options. Likewise, no 0–10 capability rating is computed in the prototype; comparison relies on physical response quantities stated above.

### Model Formulation

**Idealization.** The building is a regular multistory linear-elastic shear model with a lumped mass at each floor and lateral stiffness between floors, restricted to motion in one horizontal direction (Zuo et al., 2022).

**Mass.** Each floor's counted earthquake weight (kN) is its area times the counted load: dead load plus ψ times live load, W_i = (DL_i + ψ · LL_i) × A, and mass m_i = W_i · 1000 / g. The dead load starts at 4.8 kPa and is editable; the live load comes from the floor's use — office 2.4, residential 1.9, parking 2.4, storage 6.0 kPa, or a custom value (published summaries of NSCP 2015 Table 205-1); ψ (the counted share of live load) defaults to 0.25 and is editable per floor. All are placeholders pending engineer review. Mass therefore follows each floor's use rather than the material.

**Period and stiffness.** The empirical fundamental period T1 = Ct·H^x is evaluated with Ct = 0.072 and x = 0.8 for all materials (known placeholder weakness) with H = floors × floor height. Uniform story stiffness k delivering that period follows k = m·ω1² / (4·sin²(π / (2(2N + 1)))), with ω1 = 2π/T1. The frame factor is then applied (moment 1.0, braced 2.0, placeholder), so the empirical formula effectively describes the moment-frame case. The story stiffness matrix is tridiagonal (2k except k at the roof, −k off-diagonal).

**Damping.** Rayleigh damping C = a0·M + a1·K is calibrated to the material damping ratio (concrete 0.05, steel 0.02, composite 0.03, wood 0.05; placeholders) at the first two natural frequencies obtained from the generalized eigenproblem via mass reduction and cyclic Jacobi eigensolution, with mass-proportional fallback for a single floor. The first three periods are reported to the interface.

**Ground motion.** Three synthetic presets are generated at dt = 0.01 s with 0.5 s end tapers and then scaled so the peak sample equals the requested PGA exactly: (a) harmonic pulse at the selected frequency, (b) sum of 12 seeded in-band sinusoids over 0.2–4.0 Hz weighted toward low frequencies, and (c) half-sine impulse of 0.15 s duration with 0.2-duration lead. Defaults are harmonic, 0.30 g, 1.4 Hz, 20 s, seed 20240501.

**Time integration.** The equations M·ü + C·u̇ + K·u = −M·1·a_g(t) are stepped with Newmark average acceleration (β = 1/4, γ = 1/2) with effective stiffness factored once by Cholesky decomposition and reused each step. The solver is deterministic: identical inputs yield bit-identical outputs. Results are floor displacement histories [floor][time] in metres, summarized to per-floor peaks, building maximum, difference-of-peaks drift ratios with maximum and worst-story index, reported periods, and ground acceleration history. The 5% agreement figure in the original specification is retained only as context for comparison against a real structure; the implemented code-to-code gate is 1e-6 as described under Verification.

All coefficients, ranges, and defaults above reside in `engineeringAssumptions.ts` and are marked as placeholders. Any change requires adviser confirmation recorded in the assumptions record; components, stores, and the solver contain no hard-coded engineering numbers.

### System Architecture and Development

**Stack and execution.** React 19, TypeScript 5.9, Vite 8, Tailwind CSS 4, Zustand, Three.js with React Three Fiber, and Recharts. The engine runs in a Web Worker. The interface debounces solver requests at 150 ms, solves immediately on first paint, drops stale responses by monotonic identifier, and issues one batched request for all one-to-four buildings so comparison sides cannot race. Production entry JavaScript is held against a 220 KB gzip budget through lazy-loaded 3D, chart, and feedback chunks sharing a single 3D chunk.

**Interface.** Desktop (≥1024 px) presents controls, 3D viewport with playback bar, and results; narrower widths present one notice advising a larger screen, with an opt-in "Continue anyway" for anyone who needs it that shows the same workspace with sideways scrolling (the choice is never remembered). Controls include a seven-template picker (six starting points plus custom behavior, default 5-story concrete), a floors-and-loads tab where each floor's use and dead/live loads are set per floor with one editable live-load participation, a per-building **count-floor-loads switch** — off, the occupancy loads are not counted and every floor weighs its material's bare structure (placeholder masses pending adviser review), with the load rows kept and restored on switching back on and every derived display labeling the basis it reports — and amber use–material review flags (parking or storage on wood) that inform without blocking any edit or simulation, glossary popovers for jargon, and motion exaggeration (default ×20, 1–60) that magnifies the 3D view only with an explicit statement that numbers are unaffected. Site and ground controls are present but disabled and labeled as future scope.

**Consistency.** Calculated floor displacements drive both the 3D animation and the graphs from the same `SimulationResult`. One clock samples displacement by interpolation between engine timesteps for sway and chart cursor; animation writes to scene transforms inside the frame loop and moves the cursor by transform, causing zero React renders at 60 fps. Reduced-motion preference pauses playback on load and halves default speed.

**Comparison.** `Compare with another building` adds side B; `Add a building` extends to C then D (maximum four), defaulting to four different materials so the default four-way case compares the same building built four ways. Presentations are side-by-side (single packed row) and ghost overlay (edited building solid, others translucent), available at every count. Each building carries a fixed hue (blue, burnt orange, green, purple in both themes) and its letter A–D on its roof badge, hover callout, chart series, and table column, so color is never the sole identifier. The change column reports each building's percentage difference from baseline A with arrow and sign and states no verdict of which building is better. The controls panel keeps the Editing indicator fixed above its scrolling content, and the results panel labels whose numbers are shown with a text-plus-colour owner badge.

**Persistence and export.** `Save run` stores model, motion, and full result in IndexedDB where available and in browser storage otherwise, stating which backend is in use; the fallback thins very long runs to 2000 points while preserving peaks and periods exactly and states that frame-exact replay is unavailable for thinned runs. Runs export to one validated JSON file and import with per-run acceptance (newer-version, truncated, or wrong files refused with readable messages; partial imports name rejected runs). `Export CSV` writes full-precision histories in displayed units (mm, percent, m/s²) with a header restating headline figures exactly as displayed, sourced from the same result the charts render.

**Integrity rules.** No hard-coded engineering numbers outside the assumptions file and no hard-coded colors outside design tokens, both enforced by tests including a foreground-background contrast check against WCAG AA. The disclaimer is persistent and the Assumptions dialog content is generated from the assumptions catalogue at runtime.

### Verification Procedure

Verification is performed before any user testing in three parts.

**1. Independent-solver cross-check (built gate).** The TypeScript engine is compared with an independent Python (NumPy/SciPy) reference that shares constants via generated `constants.json` but shares no code: it solves the generalized eigenproblem with LAPACK rather than mass reduction plus Jacobi, factorizes with LAPACK Cholesky rather than a hand-rolled routine, and steps the whole matrix with NumPy. Procedure: export constants, run the shipping `simulate()` over the 26-case matrix in `cases.json` (covering 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15 and 20 floors, damping override, all three motion presets, a near-resonance case, per-floor load distributions including uniform uses and ψ at 0 and 1, and two loads-off cases whose flagged load rows must be ignored in favor of the bare-structure masses), and compare periods, peaks, drifts, maxima, roof histories (normalized root-mean-square error), and ground acceleration at tolerance 1e-6, regenerating `docs/validation-report.md` from measured numbers with non-zero exit on disagreement. Current result: PASS with worst disagreement 6.4e-11%. The 1e-6 gate is intentional: two implementations of the same arithmetic should agree to about 1e-12, and a 5% band would admit sign errors; 5% remains the appropriate order for future comparison against a real structure where modeling error is expected.

**2. Reproducibility and display consistency.** Runs with identical inputs are repeated to confirm identical outputs (guaranteed by determinism and fixed seeds). Animation and graphs are confirmed to show the same displacements by sampling the shared result array, and CSV contents are confirmed to match on-screen figures.

**3. Adviser review.** The engineering adviser reviews the final model logic, placeholder values, drift-definition difference, period-coefficient weakness, drift-band recommendation (logarithmic scale from the 84-run distribution study, reproducible via the drift-distribution script), and the explicit exclusion of site factors and any capability rating from computed results.

### Testing and Evaluation

**Parameter testing.** Testing begins from a baseline of a five-story concrete moment-frame building (14 × 10 m plan, 3.0 m story height, 0.30 g harmonic motion, 20 s). Parameters are varied one at a time under the same ground-motion input. Varied parameters include number of floors (3, 5, 10, and 15), building material (concrete, steel, composite, wood), frame type (moment, braced), PGA levels spanning 0.05–0.60 g, presets (harmonic, random, impulse), and duration. For each run the system records peak floor displacement relative to the ground, displacement over time, maximum interstory drift, worst story, natural periods, and ground acceleration. Expected trends, checked against engineering behavior within the simplification, are larger upper-floor displacement for taller buildings and reduced sway for braced relative to moment frames of otherwise identical configuration. A held-out pair from this sweep varies only the load switch: the same geometry, material, and motion simulated once with its floor loads counted and once with them off, so the bare-basis mass (a placeholder per material) is what stands behind the difference. Soil, foundation, and fault-distance variations and any 0–10 rating are not tested as computed outputs because they are not implemented.

**Software testing.** Unit and component tests (507 tests via Vitest, TypeScript↔Python agreement at a 1e-6 tolerance across 26 validation cases), end-to-end tests (125 Playwright specs including tab order, focus return, and live-region announcement, plus 5 install/offline specs against a production build), a six-width responsive sweep in light and dark themes with zero-failure requirement, and an on-device Android audit (12 of 14 walked, both failures since fixed) are executed. Coverage includes functionality across templates, per-floor loads, the loads on/off switch with bare-structure weights, loaded-versus-bare comparison, save/load/export/import including invalid-file handling and old-run migration, compatibility review flags, controls, comparison add/remove up to four sides, playback transport, theme, tour gating, and PWA install affordance behavior (hidden when installed or uninstallable, with iOS install intentionally not promised).

**Performance testing.** Entry-bundle size against the 220 KB gzip budget (measured ≈182 KB), lazy-chunk existence guards against accidental eager import of Three.js or Recharts, frame-rate behavior on common browsers and devices including lower-end targets, load time, and animation-graph consistency (Wang et al., 2025) are assessed. Simple building geometry and transform-only animation sustain interaction without render-loop state updates.

**Usability testing (instrument shipped; study not run).** Target users perform set tasks — build a model, run a simulation, and compare two configurations — and then answer the System Usability Scale questionnaire (Escaldelai et al., 2024). Informed consent is obtained; responses are anonymous and handled under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173). The SUS instrument is in the application under Feedback with parity-based scoring; TypeScript and Python scorers are cross-checked on 10 cases for exact agreement. No SUS score is reported in the repository: a usability score without participants would be a fabricated number. The client target (mean SUS ≥ 68) is assessed only after real sessions are run and exported responses are analyzed. Animation safeguards (adjustable speed, pause, loop, no rapid flashing) and accessible touch targets (44 px for icon-only controls, remainder at or above 24 px minimum) reduce participant discomfort.

### Data Analysis

Model accuracy is evaluated by percent error and root-mean-square error between QuakeStudio outputs and reference calculations, with the code-to-code gate at 1e-6 and history agreement by normalized RMSE plus reported (non-asserted) phase lag with parabolic sub-sample refinement. Parameter effects are examined through tables and graphs of peak displacement, drift, and periods against the varied parameter, with trends checked against expected engineering behavior stated above. Reproducibility is assessed by repeating identical inputs, which must yield identical outputs.

Usability data, once collected, are analyzed as task success rate, task completion time, and mean and standard deviation of SUS scores on the 0–100 scale (Escaldelai et al., 2024), with partial responses excluded rather than averaged over answered items and with samples below the minimum-participant threshold refused a study report by the analysis script. The System Usability Scale analysis implementation computes means, medians, minima, maxima, standard deviations with t-based confidence intervals, per-item means, and percentile estimates anchored at 68 as the 50th percentile. All results are interpreted as valid only within the stated model assumptions; any claim about improved learning requires a separate learning assessment (Kuncoro et al., 2023).

### Risks, Safety, and Ethics

The main risk is misinterpretation of simulation results as a structural safety assessment. Mitigations are a persistent in-application disclaimer, an assumptions page generated from the running configuration, exclusion of damage prediction and safety certification from scope and from all copy (verified by copy review that corrected four overclaim defects, including playback-bar wording), and refusal to rank buildings as better or worse.

A related risk is unrealistic results from simplified placeholders. Mitigations are adviser review, independent-solver comparison with a failing gate, documentation of all assumptions with measured limitations, and prohibition on hard-coded engineering numbers outside the reviewed file.

Technical and schedule risks are managed through one-direction linear computation, simple geometry, transform-only animation, tested offline and Android packaging (service worker disabled in the package to avoid stale precache; JDK 21 required for the Gradle build), and restriction to essential features. Participant privacy is protected through informed consent, anonymous responses, and compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Because the study involves no hazardous materials or physical experiments, safety concerns relate to data, ethics, and responsible use of results.
